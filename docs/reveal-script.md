# Reveal script: The Public Health AI and Tech Exchange

A ten-minute reveal of the Big Cities Health Coalition's use case catalog, given by Phillip Bays of Austin Public Health inside Microsoft's workshop at AIxPH 2026 in Baltimore, on Wednesday, October 14, 2026. It runs at `/reveal/`, with its speaker notes at `/reveal/notes/`.

This script is the single source for the build: on-screen words, animation and speaker lines. The deck reads its words from `src/talks/reveal/script/`, and a test (`src/talks/reveal/script/script.test.ts`) holds that copy to this page word for word.

**How to read it.** Each slide has three parts:
- **On screen:** the title, then the story lines that rise under the picture. Someone watching with no speaker should get the slide from these alone.
- **They see:** what the animation shows. One click plays the whole slide.
- **You say:** what the speaker says. It adds detail, and the slide doesn't depend on it.

**Colour cues** in They see use role names from the Austin Public Health brand: the "a person approves" mark, the "passed" tick and the process's colour. Those quoted role names are styles, not words on screen. Every other quoted phrase in They see is a label on screen.

---

## Part 1. The reveal (about 10 minutes)

### 1. Title
**On screen:** The Public Health AI and Tech Exchange
- The Big Cities Health Coalition's use case catalog, live now.

**They see:** On navy, the title, the line under it, and "Phillip Bays, Austin Public Health" under that. The live catalog's home page rises in on the right and settles.
   Labels: Phillip Bays, Austin Public Health

**You say:** Thank you, Supawit, and thank you to Microsoft for hosting this workshop. I'm Phillip Bays from Austin Public Health. I've got ten minutes, and I want to show you something the coalition launched this month.

### 2. The catalog
**On screen:** Every department's AI and data projects, in one place.
- Health departments post the AI tools, data projects and digital services they've built.
- Anyone in the coalition can find them, see how they were built and get in touch with the team.

**They see:** The catalog's home page moves to the left. One project's page lifts out of it and grows, and three of its parts light one at a time: "what it does", "what it took" and "contact".
   Labels: what it does / what it took / contact

**You say:** This is the catalog. Big city health departments post what they've built. Everyone else can find it, read how it was done and get in touch with the team that built it. Nobody has to build the same dashboard twelve times.

### 3. How it works
**On screen:** Adding a project is as easy as filling in a form.
- The form opens a request on GitHub, and scripts turn it into a draft page and check it.
- A reviewer approves it and it's live in minutes. There's no server or database to run.

**They see:** Six boxes in a row: "Form", "Request", "Draft", "Checks", "Approval" and "Live". A card moves along them. Draft and Checks take the process's colour as the card passes, and Checks gets the "passed" tick. A person stands over Approval, and the card waits there for the "a person approves" mark, then moves on, and Live lights.
   Labels: Form / Request / Draft / Checks / Approval / Live

**You say:** Here's how it works. You fill in a form. That opens a request on GitHub, and scripts turn your answers into a draft page and check it. A reviewer reads it, approves it, and a few minutes later it's live. There's no server and no database behind it, so there's nothing to pay for and nothing to patch.

### 4. How we built it
**On screen:** Austin Public Health built it with AI agents, on GitHub.
- Our team described what the coalition needed, and AI agents planned, built, tested and reviewed it.
- A person approved every change. The coalition's version was rebuilt in a weekend.

**They see:** The Austin Public Health logo, and under it three steps from left to right: "People decide what to build", "AI agents build and test it" and "A person approves the release". People stand in the first and last steps and AI agents in the middle one, and the last step gets the "a person approves" mark. Under the steps the project's record, "On GitHub", draws as a line, and each change on it gets the same mark.
   Labels: People decide what to build / AI agents build and test it / A person approves the release / On GitHub

**You say:** We built it ourselves. Our team described what the coalition needed, and AI agents planned it, built it, tested it and reviewed it. A person approved every change along the way. When the coalition wanted its own branded version, that was rebuilt in a weekend. This is how Austin Public Health builds tools now, and we'll be sharing how through the coalition's work group.

**On screen:** Post your project.
